# Denver Public Schools Ballot Issue 4A

**On ballots of Denver Public Schools electors.** The City and County of Denver and DPS share the same geographic electorate for this question. Referred by the district. This is a property-tax measure, so it is not in the city’s non-tax Ballot Information Booklet.

[Back to the summary](../ballot-summary.md)

## What a yes vote does

The certified question asks whether DPS taxes should be increased by **up to $44 million in 2026, for collection in 2027, and annually thereafter**.

Stated uses include, and are not limited to:

- educator pay
- career and technical education in high-demand fields
- student mental health
- special education

Spending would be reported in the district’s annual independent audit, published on the district website. The revenue goes to the DPS general fund, on top of other general-fund property taxes. Earnings on the proceeds would be a voter-approved revenue change under TABOR.

The mechanism is a mill levy that, **when combined with other mill-levy overrides Denver voters have already approved for the general fund**, equals the amount allowed by Colorado Revised Statutes section 22-54-108(3)(b)(VI). A yes vote fills the district up to that statutory ceiling. It is not an unlimited levy.

## What is not in the sources used here

The Denver Elections Division reprint states the $44 million cap. It does not state:

- the mill rate
- the cost per $100,000 of home value
- the cost for a typical Denver household
- how the $44 million would be split among pay, career courses, mental health, and special education
- current DPS override collections or enrollment

Those figures are not invented here. The district’s own TABOR notice, which is mailed separately from the city’s non-tax booklet, is the document that should carry the mill-levy math.

## What can swing a vote

| If this is the priority | It points toward |
| --- | --- |
| More general-fund money for pay, career courses, mental health, and special education, inside the statutory override cap, with an audit | Yes |
| Holding the DPS property-tax bill at the overrides already in place | No |
| A legally dedicated split, for example a fixed share for mental health | The ballot language says “including, but not limited to.” It does not lock percentages. If a binding split is required for your vote, this text does not provide one. |
| The dollar effect on your property | Unknown in this guide. Check the DPS notice before treating $44 million as a personal cost. $44 million is the districtwide maximum, not a household figure. |

## Polling

No public poll of Ballot Issue 4A was located.

## Relationship to statewide school measures

Proposition NN and Amendment 87 would move **state** money toward K-12. Ballot Issue 4A is a **local property tax** for DPS only. Passing or rejecting the state measures does not enact or cancel 4A.
