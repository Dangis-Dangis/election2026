# Proposition 136: Income-tax rate cap at 4.4 percent

**On every Denver ballot.** Citizen initiative. Statutory. Passes with a **majority**.

[Back to the summary](../ballot-summary.md)

Read this with [Amendment 87](amendment-87-graduated-income-tax.md).

## What the choice does

Beginning January 1, 2027, the state income-tax rate for individuals and corporations could not exceed **4.4 percent** of taxable income. That is the current rate. The measure does not cut taxes. It does not change the constitutional rule that a rate increase already has to go to voters. The legislature or a later initiative could still reduce the rate below 4.4 percent.

## If Amendment 87 also passes

Amendment 87’s rates on taxable income over $500,000 are 7.4, 7.9, and 8.4 percent. Those rates would sit above this cap. The Blue Book says the measure with more votes generally controls when two approved measures conflict, and then says the exact outcome is unclear and may take a legislative or court decision.

## Official fiscal note

No change in revenue or spending if 136 is considered alone. “The measure has no fiscal impact.”

## Arguments in the Blue Book

**For.** The cap restates voter support for the current rate, which the argument says voters approved overwhelmingly in 2022, and keeps Colorado’s rate comparatively low.

**Against.** The argument says the initiative’s function is to put Amendment 87 in legal doubt. After this election, a standalone cap does nothing that the existing voter-approval rule does not already do, because a future vote to raise the rate would override this statute.

## Where candidates and the governor stand

Axios Denver, October 22, 2026: Victor Marx supports Proposition 136. Governor Polis also supports it. Axios does not report a Weiser position on 136. Weiser’s stated silence in that article is about Amendment 87.

## What can swing a vote

| If this is the priority | It points toward |
| --- | --- |
| A statutory ceiling at today’s rate, mainly as a counter to Amendment 87 | Yes |
| A clean up-or-down vote on the graduated tax, without a second measure that may cancel the top brackets in court | No |
| An actual tax cut | This measure is not one. |
| You are voting No on Amendment 87 | 136 adds no change in the tax you pay. The “against” argument says it is then redundant. The “for” argument says the redundancy is the point, as a restatement. |
