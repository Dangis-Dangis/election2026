# Amendment 85: Plain-language ballot titles

**On every Denver ballot.** Citizen initiative. Constitutional. Passes with **55 percent**.

[Back to the summary](../ballot-summary.md)

## What the choice does

Ballot titles would have to be in plain language at no more than an eighth-grade reading level. The measure does not define who decides that a title meets the standard. It describes an eighth-grade level, in the Blue Book’s paraphrase, as short sentences, familiar words, and a conversational tone that a majority of people can understand.

It also bars laws that require extra language in **citizen-initiative** titles when that language interferes with the plain-language rule. Titles the legislature writes for its own referrals are not covered by that second ban.

Current law already tells the Title Board to use plain language and to avoid technical terms when possible. Separate statutes force specific sentences into some initiative titles, including the tax-increase formula required by TABOR and, for measures that cut revenue, an estimate of the effect on the three largest spending areas.

The Blue Book says those forced sentences are an example of language that Amendment 85 might block or put in court.

## Official fiscal note

A minimal increase in Title Board workload. No dollar figure.

## Arguments in the Blue Book

**For.** A grade-level standard is clearer than “plain language,” and it would stop a future legislature from loading citizen titles with confusing required text.

**Against.** Titles have to explain complex policies. A vague constitutional standard could multiply title challenges and could strip disclosures voters use to see tax and spending effects.

## Where candidates stand

Axios Denver, October 22, 2026, reported that Governor Jared Polis and Republican nominee Victor Marx support Amendment 85, and that Democratic nominee Phil Weiser had not taken a position. Axios noted that some opponents describe the measure as a way to strip tax disclosures. That characterization is Axios’s account of the argument, not a Blue Book finding.

## What can swing a vote

| If this is the priority | It points toward |
| --- | --- |
| Shorter titles, on the theory that more people will read them | Yes |
| Keeping the all-caps TABOR sentences that state the size of a tax increase | No |
| Fear of more lawsuits over whether a title is “eighth grade” | No |
| Stopping the legislature from adding required clauses to citizen titles only | Yes. Legislative referrals would still follow the legislature’s own drafting rules. |

This measure does not decide the tax measures. It decides how future questions are worded. Someone who wants both plain titles and full tax disclosures is in the conflict the “against” argument describes.
