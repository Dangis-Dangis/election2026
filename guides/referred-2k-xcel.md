# Referred Question 2K: Twenty-year Xcel Energy franchise

**On every Denver ballot.** Referred by City Council. The current franchise expires at the end of 2026. A yes vote approves a new contract starting **January 1, 2027**.

[Back to the summary](../ballot-summary.md)

## What a yes vote does

Public Service Company of Colorado, doing business as Xcel Energy, would keep using city streets, rights-of-way, and other city property to provide electric and gas service, under a **20-year** franchise.

From the Department of Finance note in the booklet:

- The franchise fee stays **3 percent** of Xcel’s applicable Denver electric and gas revenue. The fee is not increased.
- Customers pay bills that include this fee. Xcel remits it to the city. It is not a new city sales tax.
- **2025 actual** franchise-fee revenue: **$32.3 million**. Projection for 2026: **$32.9 million**.
- **2027** franchise-fee estimate: **$34.3 million**. Of that, **$2 million** a year goes to the Energy Efficiency Assistance Fund for low-income energy assistance and efficiency, as city code already requires. The rest goes to the general fund for services such as police, fire, parks, recreation, and libraries.
- A one-time **$2.5 million** payment in 2027 for utility assistance. Finance’s combined 2027 figure, fee plus that payment, is about **$36.9 million**.
- Undergrounding: about **$10 million a year**, equal to 1 percent of electric revenue. Xcel holds the money and undergrounds lines at the city’s direction. It is not cash to the general fund.
- Relocation: about **$150 million a year** in utility-relocation benefits. Without a franchise, the city would pay Xcel to move lines for city projects.
- Revenue moves with rates, usage, customer growth, and gas prices. Finance’s 20-year illustrations, which include the one-time payment only in 2027:

| Growth assumption | 2027 | 2037 | 2047 | 20-year total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1.5% | $36.9 million | $39.9 million | $46.3 million | $808.5 million |
| 2% (Finance’s stated 2015–2025 compound rate) | $36.9 million | $41.9 million | $51 million | $853.6 million |
| 2.5% | $36.9 million | $44 million | $56.3 million | $901.7 million |

The booklet’s general-fund table is typeset ambiguously in the text extraction. The 1.5 percent row that can be read cleanly is $34.3 million in 2027, $37.9 million in 2037, and $44.3 million in 2047. Do not treat the scrambled cells in that second table as a new official total. The total-revenue table above is the one Finance printed as a complete grid.

## Comments in the booklet

None for. None against. The clerk’s letter says blank comment sections mean nobody submitted a timely comment. They do not mean the city analyzed the contract and found no arguments.

## What news reports add

These are news accounts, not findings of the Department of Finance.

- Denverite’s September 25, 2026 guide describes additional contract terms beyond the fee: a $2.5 million upfront payment and $3.3 million over 20 years for utility affordability, both funded by Xcel; $250,000 a year for energy-resource navigators; and 1 percent of the substation-project budget for neighborhood work. Read Denverite against the franchise itself before relying on those program figures. The booklet’s fiscal note confirms the $2.5 million payment and the 1 percent undergrounding benefit. It does not, in the pages used here, itemize the $3.3 million or the $250,000.
- The Kano Project, October 6, 2026, reported that a majority of Council rejected an earlier version, that this version went to the ballot **9–2**, and that Councilwoman Shontel Lewis and then-Councilwoman Sarah Parady voted no. It reported a supporting committee, “Safe and Reliable Energy Denver,” funded almost entirely by Xcel, and spending of over **$2 million**. It reported no registered opposition committee.

## What a no vote leaves

The booklet says the current agreement expires at the end of 2026. It says that without a franchise the city would pay Xcel to relocate lines. It does not describe an interim permit, a shorter contract, or municipalization. Those alternatives are outside this text.

## What can swing a vote

| If this is the priority | It points toward |
| --- | --- |
| Keeping roughly $32 million to $34 million a year in franchise fees, plus the relocation benefit, with no gap after December 31, 2026 | Yes |
| Refusing a 20-year term, or refusing a contract whose main financial supporter on the campaign is the utility | No |
| A higher franchise fee than 3 percent, or a city-owned utility | Not offered on this question. A no vote does not create either one. |
| Low-income assistance funded by the fee and the one-time payment | Yes, to the extent those transfers depend on the agreement. The $2 million annual code transfer is described as already required by code. |
| Undergrounding at about $10 million a year, directed by the city but held by Xcel | Yes, if that program is worth the term. The money does not enter the general fund. |
