# Council District 10: what is on the November 3 ballot

District 10 does not elect a council member on November 3, 2026. Chris Hinds is the current member. The city’s council directory lists him in that seat. The next regular municipal election is in 2027. The district race is not on this ballot.

Every District 10 voter does vote for **at-large Seat B**, which is vacant. That choice, the five Denver measures in the booklet, DPS Ballot Issue 4A, Front Range rail 7A, the statewide measures, and the statewide offices are the same as in the [citywide summary](../ballot-summary.md).

What changes inside District 10 is the legislative, education, and RTD slice. Those lines do not follow the council boundary. The map below is from Denver Elections precincts in Council District 10, last edited in the city’s layer on June 12, 2024. Each precinct sits in one house district, one senate district, and one RTD district. A building on the edge of a neighborhood can fall on the other side of a line. Confirm the address at [GoVoteColorado.gov](https://www.govotecolorado.gov).

## Where the district is

The council office lists these neighborhoods: Capitol Hill, Central Business District (Upper Downtown), Cheesman Park, City Park West, Civic Center (Golden Triangle), Congress Park, Lower Downtown (LoDo), North Capitol Hill, and South City Park.

The elections precinct file uses statistical neighborhood names. For District 10 those are Capitol Hill, CBD, Cheesman Park, City Park West, Civic Center, Congress Park, North Capitol Hill, Union Station, and City Park. “Union Station” in that file is the LoDo / Union Station area. “City Park” in that file is only the part of the neighborhood inside District 10, which the council office calls South City Park. Cherry Creek and Country Club are not in these precincts. Denverite reported in 2023 that redistricting moved them to District 5, and Speer to District 7.

All 29 District 10 precincts in that file are in **Congressional District 1**. None are in District 6.

## Where District 10 splits

Area shares are the share of precinct land area, not of registered voters. A small neighborhood can hold more voters than a large one. Do not treat the percentages as a turnout forecast.

### State House

| If your precinct neighborhood is | House district | Candidates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capitol Hill, North Capitol Hill, Cheesman Park, Congress Park | 6 | Iris Halpern (D), Kathleen Angel (R) |
| CBD, Civic Center, Union Station | 5 | Justine Sandoval (D), Johnnie W. Johnson (R) |
| City Park West, and the District 10 portion of City Park | 8 | Lindsay Gilchrist (D), Carolyn Barnes (R), Jesse Lashawn Parris (Unity) |

About 56 percent of District 10’s precinct area is House District 6, 25 percent is House District 5, and 18 percent is House District 8. No public poll of any of these three races was located. Issue positions were not compiled. See the [legislative guide](state-legislature.md).

Denverite’s July 1, 2026 primary story, written on election night, described House District 6 as stretching east from Capitol Hill along Colfax, and House District 5 as covering a stretch of west and north Denver. Those descriptions match this split: the Colfax neighborhoods are District 6, and downtown / Union Station are District 5. That story’s vote counts were not a canvass. They are not repeated here as certified results.

### State Senate

| If your precinct neighborhood is | Senate district | Candidates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every District 10 neighborhood except Union Station | 31 | Matt Ball (D). No other candidate is on the sample ballot. |
| Union Station | 34 | Chela Garcia Irlando (D), Jennifer Qualteri (R), Jacob Luria (L) |

About 92 percent of the precinct area is Senate District 31. The Union Station precincts are the exception, about 8 percent of the area, and they are Senate District 34. The division’s candidate table spells the Republican nominee Qualteri. Use the ballot spelling.

| If your precinct neighborhood is | RTD district | On this ballot? |
| --- | --- | --- |

About 74 percent of District 10 precinct area is RTD District A and has no RTD contest. About 26 percent is District C.

## What can swing a District 10 ballot

The citywide measures do not change because of the council district. The swing tables in those guides still apply. The district only changes which names sit in the legislative and transit slots.

| If this is the priority | It points toward |
| --- | --- |
| A council member for District 10 | Not this election. Hinds remains the member until the 2027 municipal election. The citywide choice on this ballot is at-large Seat B. |
| The Democrat who won the District 1 primary | Kiros, for every District 10 voter. DeGette is not on the general-election ballot. |
| A state House vote | Whichever row matches the neighborhood table. Voting in the wrong district’s guide will not match the ballot. |
| Senate control, for most of the district | There is no opponent to Ball on the Senate District 31 ballot. Union Station voters do have a three-way Senate District 34 race. |
| An RTD director | Only City Park West, the District 10 part of City Park, and Union Station. Elsewhere in the district, skip that section of the sample ballot. It is printed there because the sample includes every Denver style. |
| Rail tax versus the RTD board | Ballot Issue 7A is separate from the RTD seat. District A voters still vote on 7A. |

## Data gaps

The area percentages are land area inside the precincts, not voters. The precinct neighborhood name can disagree with a resident’s own name for the block. The school-board district field in the precinct file is empty. No poll of the District 1 House race, or of House Districts 5, 6, or 8, was located.
