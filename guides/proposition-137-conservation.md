# Proposition 137: Sporting-goods sales tax for conservation

**On every Denver ballot.** Citizen initiative. Statutory. Passes with a **majority**.

[Back to the summary](../ballot-summary.md)

Read this with [Proposition NN](proposition-nn-k12-tabor.md).

## What the choice does

The sales-tax rate does not change, and the price of sporting goods does not change. The measure takes the state’s share of sales tax on sporting goods and rentals and spends it on named outdoor purposes, outside the TABOR limit.

Sporting goods in the Blue Book’s description include bicycles, golf equipment, hunting and fishing equipment, camping equipment, boats, recreational vehicles, and campers, plus rentals of recreational, sports, and fitness equipment.

Legislative Council would calculate the amount. For FY 2027–28 the estimate is **$175 million**, then **$180.2 million** in FY 2028–29, growing later with sales. In some years the transfer is reduced so that two income-tax credits for low- and moderate-income households are not cut.

Estimated split in FY 2027–28:

| Use | Amount | Share |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Great Outdoors Colorado | up to $83.1 million | 47.5% |
| Colorado State Forest Service | up to $36.5 million | part of the 47.5% wildfire and watershed share |
| Department of Natural Resources | up to $36.6 million | part of that same share |
| Department of Public Safety, one time, prescribed-fire damages | $10.0 million | inside the wildfire share for this year only |
| Colorado Parks and Wildlife | up to $4.4 million | 2.5% |
| Office of Economic Development and International Trade | up to $4.4 million | 2.5% |

The Blue Book says the outdoor purposes covered here now receive about **$114 million**, of which GOCO’s lottery funding is about $90 million and is not tax funding. The $175 million would be additional to that narrower set of programs. The measure says the new money must supplement, not replace, existing wildfire and conservation funding. That “supplement not supplant” line is from the separate fiscal-impact statement dated September 10, 2026.

## TABOR

In a year over the limit, refunds fall by the amount retained. Using the June 2026 forecast, staff expects refunds to drop by **$175 million** in tax year 2027, enough to eliminate the six-tier sales-tax refund in at least FY 2027–28: about **$26 to $83** per single filer and **$52 to $166** per joint filer, depending on income.

In a year under the limit, there is no refund to cut. The $175 million instead comes out of the general fund, whose three largest uses are health care, education, and human services. The legislature would decide what gives.

## Interaction with Proposition NN

NN retains money above the TABOR limit. Proposition 137 shrinks the money subject to that limit. If both pass, NN’s school-and-children retention gets smaller and can disappear in a given year. Proposition 137 does not change NN’s legal ceiling.

## Arguments in the Blue Book

**For.** Wildfire, watersheds, parks, and access get a dedicated stream without a higher tax rate. The argument calls this a user-based model because people who buy sporting goods are more likely to use the outdoors. It cites unreliable federal support and pressure on the regular budget.

**Against.** Keeping money that would have been refunded is a tax increase in effect. Dollars kept here are dollars not refunded and not available for health care or education. The argument says GOCO and similar programs already have dedicated sources and that the legislature should keep the flexibility to move money.

## Where the governor candidates stand

Axios Denver, October 22, 2026: Phil Weiser supports Proposition 137. Victor Marx opposes it, together with Proposition NN, because of the effect on TABOR refunds.

## What can swing a vote

| If this is the priority | It points toward |
| --- | --- |
| A dedicated outdoor, wildfire, and watershed fund at no change in the tax rate on gear | Yes |
| Protecting the six-tier sales-tax refund, on the order of $26 to $166 in the year staff estimated | No |
| Protecting general-fund health care and education in a below-the-limit year | No |
| You are also voting Yes on NN for schools | 137 reduces NN’s practical yield. If the school retention is the higher priority, that is a reason to vote No on 137 or to accept a smaller NN. |
| Trust in GOCO’s existing lottery governance versus a new earmark the legislature cannot reallocate | Yes if you want the earmark; No if you want annual legislative control |
