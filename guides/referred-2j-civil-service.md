# Referred Question 2J: Civil Service Commission and Department of Safety

**On every Denver ballot.** Referred by City Council. Charter amendment. In the non-tax booklet.

[Back to the summary](../ballot-summary.md)

## What a yes vote does

The ballot title names three goals: remove outdated language, let Council add duties in the municipal code, and extend probation when medical, military, or other approved leave requires it. The charter text does more than those three phrases. The changes below are from the booklet’s strikeout text and the “for” comment.

**Probation.** Original police and fire appointments include academy time plus nine months, with stated exceptions. If during probation someone takes approved leave of **30 calendar days or more**, including military leave, leave under the ADA or FMLA, or leave the chief approves, probation may be extended up to **six more months** after they return. The Manager of Safety may still dismiss a probationary member without cause, and that dismissal is not appealable.

**Command caps removed.** The charter today says fire command assignments under section 9.5.2 “shall not exceed seven,” and police command assignments under section 9.6.3 “shall not exceed eighteen.” Both sentences are deleted. A separate sentence stays in the police section: the chief must make reasonable efforts not to cause a net increase in command staff, including lieutenants and captains, or a net decrease in line positions. Pay for the fire command assignments would be set by ordinance.

**Civil Service Commission membership.** The requirement that commissioners be United States citizens is removed. The “for” comment states that removal directly. Appointment structure stays: five members, two named by the mayor, two by Council, one nominated by the mayor and appointed by Council. Terms stay two years. Appointers must consider the diversity of Denver residents. Gendered pronouns are replaced.

**Cadet program.** A public-safety cadet program is placed under the Manager of Safety, who sets terms and qualifications. Cadets are not permanent employees and may be dismissed at the manager’s discretion. Cadet time does not count as classified service. The old commission section on the cadet program is left in the printed text as well; the “for” comment says the program moves to the Manager of Safety article.

**Discipline timelines.** The manager’s review of a chief’s disciplinary order changes from 20 business days to **15 calendar days**. Suspension pending investigation, before charges are filed, changes from 10 days to **20 business days**. Service rules that specified certified mail are replaced with “in a manner prescribed by ordinance.”

**Other.** Council may, when the commission asks, add duties by ordinance. Longevity-pay section 9.4.10 is marked repealed in the strikeout. Several reserved sections are renumbered or cleared. Lateral-hire pay rules for experienced police and fire applicants are restated in the text.

The “for” comment says the underlying rules date to 1904 and have been amended in pieces.

## Official fiscal note

Finance says dropping the command caps has a **de minimis** cost if authorized strength does not change, because command pay has historically moved with the bargaining unit and a larger command group would be a management choice. The note does not price the probation, citizenship, or timeline changes.

## Comments

**For.** Modernization, gender-neutral language, leave-adjusted probation, flexibility as the city grows, and removal of the citizenship requirement for commissioners. The comment says this is not an overhaul.

**Against.** None filed.

A news account of the referral, the Kano Project’s October 6, 2026 summary of council action, says Council approved the question unanimously on the consent agenda in August and that no committees had registered for or against it. That is a report about the political lineup, not a fiscal finding.

## What can swing a vote

| If this is the priority | It points toward |
| --- | --- |
| Probation should pause for long medical or military leave instead of running out while someone is gone | Yes |
| The hard caps of 7 fire and 18 police command assignments should stay in the charter | No |
| Non-citizens should be eligible for the five-member Civil Service Commission | Yes |
| Commission membership should remain limited to U.S. citizens | No |
| Faster mayoral review of discipline (15 calendar days) and a longer window before charges must be filed (20 business days) | Yes, if you want those specific timeline swaps. They move in opposite directions. |
| A single “cleanup” with no staffing effect | The fiscal note supports that only if authorized strength is unchanged. The caps coming out of the charter are a real legal change even if nobody adds a job in 2027. |
