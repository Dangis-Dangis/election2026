# Amendment 82: Right to purchase and sell natural gas

**On every Denver ballot.** Citizen initiative. Constitutional. Passes with **55 percent**.

[Back to the summary](../ballot-summary.md)

## What the choice does

A yes vote adds a constitutional right for consumers to purchase natural gas for cooking or heating in homes or businesses, and a right for distributors and utilities to sell it for that use.

The Blue Book states:

- Natural gas is primarily methane. It fuels furnaces, water heaters, dryers, stoves, and ovens. Electricity and propane can power the same appliances.
- Investor-owned gas utilities are regulated by the Public Utilities Commission, including rates, safety, and clean-heat plans. In December 2025 the PUC required plans for a **41 percent** cut in greenhouse-gas emissions by 2035 from 2015 levels, matching targets already in state law.
- Local governments may regulate installation and repair of gas service and appliances. Some have limited gas appliances in new construction. The Blue Book’s example is Crested Butte, which requires electricity for heating, water heating, and other appliances in new homes.
- The amendment “may limit the ability for state or local governments to enforce existing or future regulations related to natural gas.” It does not, by its own text, repeal a named Denver ordinance. Whether a particular Denver building rule would fall is a legal question the Blue Book does not answer.

## Official fiscal note

Possible small costs for the Department of Local Affairs to revise advice on building codes, and more legal work for the PUC, the Department of Natural Resources, the Department of Public Health and Environment, and the Colorado Energy Office. Cities with gas restrictions may have legal and permitting costs to conform their codes. No dollar estimate is given.

## Arguments in the Blue Book

**For.** Gas is a widely used heating source, including in extreme cold, and the amendment is written to stop state or local government from cutting off the ability to buy it.

**Against.** Gas emits greenhouse gases and contributes to air pollution. A constitutional right would constrain emission rules and local health or climate choices, including a shift to wind, solar, or geothermal.

## What can swing a vote

| If this is the priority | It points toward |
| --- | --- |
| Keeping the option to buy gas for heat and cooking, including against future bans | Yes |
| Leaving room for city building codes and the PUC’s clean-heat rules to restrict new gas hookups | No |
| Putting an energy entitlement in the constitution, which takes 55 percent to adopt and another vote to undo | Yes, if you want that lock-in; No, if you want the rule to stay in statute and local code |
| Denver-specific construction policy | Unresolved. The Blue Book does not say which Denver rules, if any, would be invalid. |
