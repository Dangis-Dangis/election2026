[Pollitrack’s](https://pollitrack.com/co) October 2026 writeup says Hickenlooper won the June Democratic primary over state Sen. Julie Gonzales with 52.8 percent, and that Baisley was unopposed for the Republican nomination. That site is a model and narrative project. The primary percentages should be checked against the Secretary of State’s certified primary results if the exact figure will decide something for you. The general-election names above are from the clerk’s general-election list.

Hickenlooper’s 2020 general-election margin over Cory Gardner, 9.3 points, is also stated by Pollitrack. It is a prior election, not a 2026 poll.

## Polling

No statewide public poll of Hickenlooper versus Baisley was located. Pollitrack said none had been published as of October 24, 2026, and that its own “Hickenlooper ahead by 11” figure is a stand-in built from the 2024 presidential margin, not a survey.

Do not import these district polls into a Denver decision:

- Impact Research, September 21–24, 2026, 403 likely voters in the **4th Congressional District** (the Eastern Plains and Douglas County, not Denver): Baisley 50, Hickenlooper 45.
- Nothing in the 5th District poll reviewed for the governor’s race was a Senate horse race for Denver.

Colorado Pols’ “Big Line,” updated October 22, 2026, gives Hickenlooper a 98 percent insider chance and Baisley 2 percent. The site says the numbers are not a forecast of the margin and are not a poll.

## What can swing a vote

Issue platforms for all six ballot candidates were not compiled. Inventing a contrast would be worse than leaving the gap. The structural choices that are documented:

| If this is the priority | It points toward |
| --- | --- |
| Reelecting the Democratic incumbent | Hickenlooper |
| Replacing him with the Republican nominee | Baisley |
| A minor party as a protest or a specific platform | Chew, Baum, Huber, or Withrow, after you read that candidate’s own materials. This guide does not summarize them. |
| National Senate control | Colorado’s seat is one vote in a chamber this guide does not model. No public statewide poll here shows the Colorado seat as close. The 4th District poll cannot fill that gap. |
