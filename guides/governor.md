# Governor and lieutenant governor

**On every Denver ballot.** One vote for a ticket. Governor Jared Polis is term-limited and is not a candidate.

[Back to the summary](../ballot-summary.md)

## Who is on the ballot

From the Denver sample ballot, in ballot order:

| Governor | Lieutenant governor | Party |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Victor Marx | George Washington Markert | Republican |
| Phil Weiser | Lesley Dahlkemper | Democratic |
| Stephen T. Hamilton | James K. Treibert | American Constitution |
| Eric Mulder | Wayne Harlos | Libertarian |
| Jeff Peckman | T.J. Cole | Unity |
| Erik Underwood | Frank Atwood | Approval Voting |
| Greg Lopez | Taralyn Romero | Unaffiliated |

Weiser is the current attorney general. Marx is described in news accounts as a Marine veteran and ministry leader. Lopez is a former Republican member of Congress running unaffiliated. Those identifications are from MultiState’s October 19, 2026 race page and the sample ballot. This guide does not rank the minor-party tickets. Their platforms were not compiled.

Decision Desk HQ’s October 28, 2026 forecast page gave Weiser a 93 percent win probability and a modeled D+20 margin, and said the model was 86 percent fundamentals, 3 percent polls, and 11 percent markets. That is a model, not a poll. MultiState’s October 19 page said markets were about 96 to 97 percent for the Democrat and that Cook, Sabato, and Pluribus averaged to a Solid Democratic rating. Colorado Pols’ October 22 insider sheet put Weiser at 90 percent, Marx at 5, and Lopez at 5, and said the percentages are not margins.

## Documented differences between Weiser and Marx

Only contrasts found in named sources are listed. Silence is not a position.

From MultiState’s account of an October 8, 2026 Colorado Chamber forum:

- Weiser spoke about building 40,000 starter homes, down-payment aid, K-12 funding, youth mental health, and reforming TABOR. He said wealthy residents can pay “a little more.” He said he would sign a bill easing the Labor Peace Act’s second union election, and he defended targeted emissions rules.
- Marx spoke about faster housing approvals, cost-benefit review before new regulations, auditing agencies, and no new taxes. He said he would veto that labor bill, keep using natural gas, and explore small nuclear reactors.

Those forum lines are MultiState’s report of the event. They are not a transcript in this folder.

## What can swing a vote

| If this is the priority | It points toward |
| --- | --- |
| A Democratic attorney general who supports NN and 137, has not taken a side on the graduated tax, and told a business forum he would ease the second union election | Weiser |
| A Republican nominee who opposes NN, 137, and the graduated tax, supports the 4.4 percent cap and the plain-language and redistricting amendments, and said he would veto the labor bill | Marx |
| An unaffiliated former Republican member of Congress rather than either major-party nominee | Lopez. His ballot-measure positions were not in the Axios roundup. |
| The summer 2025 statewide poll, or a national rating, as proof of the November margin | Neither. The only statewide horse-race poll is from 2025. The 2026 polls that look close were taken in the 4th and 5th districts. |
| A minor party | Hamilton, Mulder, Peckman, or Underwood. Read their own materials. This guide does not supply positions. |

Lieutenant governor candidates are elected only as part of the ticket. A vote for Weiser is a vote for Dahlkemper. A vote for Marx is a vote for Markert.
