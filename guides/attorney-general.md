# Attorney General

**On every Denver ballot.** Vote for one. Phil Weiser, the current attorney general, is the Democratic nominee for governor and is not running for this office.

## Who is on the ballot

| Candidate | Party |
| --- | --- |
| Michael J. Allen | Republican |
| Jena Griswold | Democratic |

Griswold is the current secretary of state. She was still in that office on September 4, 2026, when her office certified the ballot.

The sample ballot does not list a minor-party candidate for attorney general.

## Polling

No public horse-race poll of Griswold versus Allen was located.

Colorado Pols’ Big Line, updated October 22, 2026, is an unscientific insider sheet, not a poll. It rated this the most competitive statewide race still held by a Democrat, at 60 percent for Griswold and 40 percent for Allen, and said national Republicans had looked at the race and then “lost interest.” The site says its percentages are not predicted margins. Treat that as a tip sheet from one outlet.

## What can swing a vote

The attorney general represents the state in court, issues legal opinions, and runs consumer, criminal, and civil-rights units defined in statute. Several 2026 measures would create legal work for that office no matter who wins:

- Amendment 87 and Proposition 136, if both pass, leave what the Blue Book calls an unclear conflict over the top income-tax rates.
- Proposition 134’s relationship to the Colorado Anti-Discrimination Act is “unclear” in the Blue Book.
- Amendment 82 may produce litigation over gas regulations.
- Amendment 81 changes what local officers must tell federal immigration authorities.

This guide did not find a sourced, side-by-side statement of how Allen and Griswold would handle those cases. Party is the documented difference. It is not a substitute for their own statements on criminal prosecution, consumer protection, or the ballot measures.
