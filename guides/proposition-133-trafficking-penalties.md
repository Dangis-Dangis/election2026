# Proposition 133: Penalties for human trafficking of a minor

**On every Denver ballot.** Citizen initiative. Statutory. Passes with a **majority**.

[Back to the summary](../ballot-summary.md)

## What the choice does

Under current Colorado law, human trafficking of a minor for sexual servitude is making a minor available for commercial sexual activity for money or other compensation. A minor cannot consent to that activity. The crime is a **Class 2 felony**: **8 to 48 years** in prison and a mandatory **5 years** of parole. Judges still select a sentence inside that range. Current law includes defenses for people who are themselves trafficking victims.

A yes vote:

- Raises the crime to a **Class 1 felony** punished by **life in prison without parole**.
- Adds buying or selling sexual activity with a minor, in exchange for anything of monetary value, to the crime. Buyers and sellers both face the life sentence if convicted.
- Removes judicial discretion in the sentence.

## Official fiscal note

Extra prison cost does not start until affected people reach the point at which they would have been paroled under today’s term. Staff puts the first cost at about **$180,000 in FY 2041–42**, then growing by about that amount for several years ($360,000, then $540,000, and so on), not adjusted for inflation. Costs later level off as life sentences end by death, commutation, or pardon. Medical costs rise separately as people age in prison. If more people are convicted because the crime is broader, costs are higher than this estimate. Trial workload grows slightly. General Fund is the assumed source.

## Arguments in the Blue Book

**For.** The harm to children justifies a life sentence rather than a term that can include the low end of today’s range or probation. The argument says a fixed sentence is the deterrence and the protection.

**Against.** The argument says harsher sentences are unlikely to deter trafficking and may punish victims whom traffickers force into criminal acts. Fear of a life sentence may reduce reporting and cooperation. Judges could not weigh the facts. The argument prefers prevention resources over a mandatory life term.

## What can swing a vote

| If this is the priority | It points toward |
| --- | --- |
| Anyone convicted of this expanded crime should be in prison for life, with no parole and no judicial reduction | Yes |
| An 8-to-48-year range still allows a life-length sentence in the worst cases without requiring it in every case | No |
| Buyers, not only people who deliver a minor to someone else, should be inside the trafficking statute at this penalty | Yes |
| Victim-defendants need a sentence a judge can fit to coercion | No |
| Near-term budget | The staff cost is small until about 2041. Budget pressure is a weak near-term swing and a real long-term one if conviction volume rises. |
