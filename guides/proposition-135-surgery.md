# Proposition 135: Surgery on minors in response to perception of sex or gender

**On every Denver ballot.** Citizen initiative. Statutory. Passes with a **majority**.

[Back to the summary](../ballot-summary.md)

This guide uses the Blue Book’s clinical description of a medical-policy question. It does not describe procedures.

## What the choice does

A yes vote prohibits surgery on a minor when the purpose is to alter biological sex characteristics as a treatment in response to the minor’s perception of sex or gender. The ban is based on purpose, not on a list of named operations. The Blue Book says the covered care can include procedures that change reproductive organs, body shape, facial appearance, or voice, and that the purpose test might also limit some surgeries for minors who are not transgender.

Allowed under the measure, as the Blue Book states them:

- male circumcision
- treatment of medically verifiable disorders of sex development
- treatment of acquired physical or chemical abnormalities
- non-surgical care, including counseling and social transition

A yes vote also prohibits state or federal funds, Medicaid reimbursement, and insurance coverage from paying for the prohibited surgeries. Current law allows medically necessary gender- and sex-based surgery to be covered by private insurance, public programs, or personal payment, and the Blue Book says Colorado law protects access.

## What the Blue Book says about how often this happens

Staff says it is unknown how many minors in Colorado receive surgery this measure would ban.

A study the Blue Book cites, using 2019 insurance claims and researchers at Harvard, estimated that for every 100,000 youths ages 15 to 17 who were identified in the study’s transgender or gender-diverse group, **2.1** had at least one gender-affirming surgery. For ages 13 to 14 the estimate is **0.1 per 100,000**. The researchers found no cases at age 12 or younger in that dataset. Those are national claim rates, not a Colorado census.

The Blue Book reports that Children’s Hospital Colorado, Rocky Mountain Children’s, and Denver Health **say they do not perform** gender- or sex-based surgeries on transgender patients under 18.

## Official fiscal note

Minimal administrative cost. Possible small savings in Medicaid and public-employee insurance if these surgeries are no longer covered. Staff expects any savings to be minimal, consistent with the rarity finding.

## Arguments in the Blue Book

**For.** Minors should not make permanent surgical decisions. Non-surgical care can continue until adulthood. The argument says long-term evidence is incomplete and that some people regret surgery. It notes that other states have limited surgical care for minors.

**Against.** The purpose test is broader than a ban on one class of transgender surgery and can make insurers and doctors drop other care. The argument says decisions belong with the patient, parents, and clinicians, and that cutting off surgical care may worsen anxiety, depression, and suicide risk for transgender youth.

The two arguments disagree on both the medical evidence and how far the legal text reaches. The staff section does not pick a winner. It does say the three large pediatric providers report they are not performing these surgeries on transgender patients under 18.

## What can swing a vote

| If this is the priority | It points toward |
| --- | --- |
| No surgery of this purpose before 18, even if a parent and a doctor agree, and no insurance payment for it | Yes |
| Those decisions should stay with families and clinicians, under current Colorado access rules | No |
| The ban’s practical effect on transgender youth in the three named hospital systems | Those systems already report they do not perform the surgeries. A yes vote still changes the legal rule and the insurance rule, including for care outside those systems. |
| Fear that a purpose test will be used to deny unrelated surgery | No, until the text is narrowed. The Blue Book flags that uncertainty and does not resolve it. |
| Counseling and non-surgical care | Not prohibited by this measure. |
