# Judicial retention

Retention is a yes or no on each judge, not a race between candidates. A judge who does not receive a majority of yes votes leaves the bench. The Denver candidate list and the sample ballot are the roster. Not every judge is on every ballot. The Blue Book says most readers will not see every judge printed in the statewide book.

[Back to the summary](../ballot-summary.md)

## How the state says to decide

The Blue Book’s introduction says retention information comes from the commissions on judicial performance, and that the commissions’ explanations are in the Blue Book and at [judicialperformance.colorado.gov](https://judicialperformance.colorado.gov). Commissions look at survey data from lawyers and non-lawyers who appeared before the judge, and at the judge’s self-evaluation and record. They recommend retain or do not retain. A recommendation is not a requirement. Voters may vote no on a judge the commission says to retain, and the reverse.

This project’s text extract of the Blue Book did **not** include the individual narratives or the retain / do-not-retain line for each Denver judge. Those lines are not guessed here.

## What can swing a yes or no

| If this is the priority | It points toward |
| --- | --- |
| Following the performance commission unless you have a separate, specific reason | Use the commission line on that judge at judicialperformance.colorado.gov or in the printed Blue Book. This guide does not supply the line. |
| A single “no” on every judge, or a single “yes” on every judge, as a protest or a show of support for the system | That is a coherent rule only if you mean it to ignore the per-judge record. The commissions exist because the records differ. |
| Disagreement with a written opinion | Supreme Court and Court of Appeals retention is where that priority applies. Trial-court judges are evaluated more on courtroom conduct, case management, and the survey results than on a statewide policy vote. |
| Party | Retention ballots do not print a party. Colorado judges are appointed, then stand for retention. |

## Polling

Retention questions are not horse races. No Denver retention poll was located, and a horse-race poll would not be the right evidence. The commission surveys are the official quantitative record.
