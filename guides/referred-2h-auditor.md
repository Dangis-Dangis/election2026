# Referred Question 2H: Auditor enforcement of wage and workplace laws

**On every Denver ballot.** Referred by City Council. Charter amendment. In the 2026 Ballot Information Booklet because it has no tax effect.

[Back to the summary](../ballot-summary.md)

## What a yes vote does

The charter would state, in a new section 5.2.1(F):

> The Auditor shall enforce wage, worker protection, and workplace rights, as the Council by ordinance may require.

Existing subsections on other duties and the deputy auditor would be relettered. The measure does not, in that sentence, create a new city department or a new wage rate. Ordinances would still define the duties.

## Official fiscal note

The Department of Finance: “There is no anticipated fiscal impact … as this function is currently being performed by the Auditor’s Office.”

## Comments in the booklet

Clerk Paul D. López’s letter says the office prints timely comments, summarizes them to the 500-word limit, and does not edit them further or verify their claims.

**For.** The submitted comments say:

- The Auditor has enforced the prevailing-wage ordinance since 1950, and since 2016 has had additional duties on minimum wage and wage theft.
- One comment says the office has collected about **$10 million** in restitution and enforced the rights of more than **15,000** workers in the last decade.
- A later comment says that in the last five years the Auditor collected more than **$9 million** and vindicated more than **15,000** workers across thousands of investigations.
- The comments say the people most often harmed by wage theft are women, people of color, immigrants, young workers, and low-income people, and that the office is a no-cost path for people who cannot hire a lawyer.
- They say the amendment does not expand authority. It matches the charter to current ordinance and practice, so a future dispute cannot unsettle enforcement.

**Against.** “No comments were submitted against this measure.”

The two restitution figures do not match each other. Both are unverified comments. This guide does not choose between $10 million over a decade and more than $9 million over five years.

## What can swing a vote

| If this is the priority | It points toward |
| --- | --- |
| The charter should name a power the city says the Auditor already uses, so enforcement of wage ordinances is explicit | Yes |
| Wage enforcement should stay in ordinances only, without a new charter duty that Council fills in later | No |
| Cost | The fiscal note says zero. Cost is not a reason to vote No unless you reject that note. |
| A larger Auditor budget or a new wage law | Not in this question. A yes vote does not appropriate money or set a wage. |
| Absence of a filed opposition comment | That is a fact about the comment process. It is not evidence that no one objects. |
