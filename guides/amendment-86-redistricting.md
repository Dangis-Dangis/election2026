# Amendment 86: Off-cycle congressional redistricting

**On every Denver ballot.** Citizen initiative. Constitutional. Passes with **55 percent**.

[Back to the summary](../ballot-summary.md)

## What the choice does

Colorado draws congressional districts once a decade through the Independent Congressional Redistricting Commission created by voters in 2018. The next regular redraw is in 2031. The current constitution does not spell out a process for changing districts in between.

Amendment 86 would require any off-cycle change to be approved by that commission and adopted by the Colorado Supreme Court. The commission would have to hold at least three public hearings. Changes could not purposefully favor a political party or unnecessarily divide communities of interest. Population figures would have to come from the most recent census or another data source.

The Blue Book says that since 2025 at least ten states have redrawn congressional maps outside the census cycle. It does not say Colorado has done so.

Using the 2020 census population of 5.8 million, the Blue Book states the current target district size is 721,714 people.

## Official fiscal note

About **$370,000** in the Legislative Department in any year the commission is called in off cycle, plus a small judicial workload. No cost if no off-cycle map is proposed.

## Arguments in the Blue Book

**For.** Public hearings and a party-neutral rule would extend the 2018 commission system to the mid-decade redraws happening in other states.

**Against.** The same commission, and a new constitutional procedure, would stand in the way if Colorado voters later wanted a mid-decade response to maps elsewhere, or believed the 2021 map no longer fit. The argument says voters, not that commission, should have the final word.

## Where candidates stand

Axios Denver, October 22, 2026: Phil Weiser opposes Amendment 86. Jared Polis and Victor Marx support it.

## What can swing a vote

| If this is the priority | It points toward |
| --- | --- |
| Making a mid-decade Colorado map go through the independent commission, three hearings, and the Supreme Court | Yes |
| Preserving a simpler path for the legislature or the voters to redraw if other states’ mid-decade maps change the national balance | No |
| The cost | Only matters if an off-cycle redraw is actually attempted. The standing cost is about zero. |
| Denver’s House seat | District 1 and the Denver portion of District 6 were drawn in 2021. This amendment does not move those lines by itself. |
