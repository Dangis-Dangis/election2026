# Initiated Ordinance 311: Force-feeding birds and the sale of foie gras

**On every Denver ballot.** Citizen initiative. Ordinance, not a charter change. In the non-tax booklet. Effective **July 1, 2027** if it passes.

[Back to the summary](../ballot-summary.md)

## What a yes vote does

The ordinance would:

- Prohibit force-feeding a bird to enlarge its liver beyond normal size. A veterinarian may tube-feed a bird to improve its health.
- Prohibit food-service and retail businesses from selling or giving a fattened bird-liver product, alone or as an ingredient. The sale occurs where the recipient takes possession.
- Prohibit importing, transporting, or receiving such a product in Denver for sale or other commercial use, no matter where it was produced.
- Leave personal possession and personal consumption unaddressed by the “for” comment, which says the ordinance does not restrict them. The operative ban is on sale, commercial receipt, and force-feeding. Read section 8-162 of the text before assuming a dinner at home is regulated.
- Assign enforcement to the Denver Department of Public Health and Environment, including routine retail inspections, subpoenas for poultry-purchase records, a complaint process, and a public database of violations updated quarterly.
- Set a civil penalty of **$1,000 to $5,000 per violation**. Each day of a continuing violation is a separate offense. Repeat violations within 12 months require suspension of the business license for up to six months. The city attorney may also seek an injunction. Owners and operators can be penalized for a corporate violation.

“Fattened bird liver products” in the text include foie gras and also duck fat, pâtés, spreads, and other derivatives of the fattened liver. The “for” comment describes the target as foie gras from force-fed birds and says foie gras produced without force-feeding is not restricted. The definition section is broader than the comment’s summary. That gap matters if a product is made from a fattened liver but is not labeled foie gras.

## Official fiscal note

Finance, after consulting the health department, anticipates **no fiscal impact**. Any added inspection work “would be minimal and could be absorbed by existing employees.”

## Comments

The clerk does not verify comments. No against comment was filed.

**For, as submitted.** Force-feeding is described as pumping corn and oil through a tube for weeks so the liver swells to about ten times normal size, with broken beaks, inability to stand, and denial of water for bathing. The comment says wild birds’ livers swell to about 1.5 times before migration. It says the product is a luxury, not a staple, and not a primary revenue source for any business. It says the practice is illegal in more than twenty countries, including most of the European Union, and in New York City, Pittsburgh, Portland, and California. It compares the ordinance to Denver’s ban on retail pet sales. It says the petition used volunteers, not paid circulators.

The comment also says: “A Colorado State University poll found that 85% of Colorado voters support ending the sale of foie gras from farms where force-feeding is used.” The clerk did not verify that sentence. This guide did not locate the poll instrument, the sample, or the question wording. **Do not treat 85 percent as an established survey result.**

## What can swing a vote

| If this is the priority | It points toward |
| --- | --- |
| Ending the local sale of a product the “for” comment describes as the result of force-feeding, at no expected city cost | Yes |
| Keeping restaurants free to sell a legal imported food, with welfare rules set where the birds are raised | No |
| Daily fines of up to $5,000 and license suspension | Those penalties are in the text. They point toward No if the fine schedule is the objection, even if you dislike the production method. |
| A ban limited to products labeled foie gras | The definition also names duck fat, pâtés, and other derivatives of the fattened liver. If you want a narrower ban, this text is broader than the campaign summary. |
| Personal consumption at home of something already purchased | The “for” comment says that is not restricted. The enforcement target is commercial sale and force-feeding. |
