# Proposition 134: Male and female participation in school and college sports

**On every Denver ballot.** Citizen initiative. Statutory. Passes with a **majority**.

[Back to the summary](../ballot-summary.md)

## What the choice does

Every school-based team or sport, from elementary school through college, would have to be designated:

- biological males only
- biological females only
- or coeducational / mixed

Students would compete on the team matching biological sex, or on a coed team. A biological female may compete on a male team if no female team is offered. The measure does not use a required medical test. Each school or district writes its own verification policy.

“Male” and “female” are defined by whether the person’s biological reproductive system is organized around sperm or ova.

Coverage includes public, charter, private, and religious schools; intramurals; and school-sponsored after-school sports. It includes public and private colleges. It does **not** include youth leagues that are separate from schools.

For public K-12, the education commissioner must notify a noncompliant district and then take corrective action if the district does not make a good-faith effort. The measure states no enforcement tool for private K-12, colleges, or athletic associations. It bars government, accreditors, and athletic associations from penalizing a school for keeping a female-only designation.

## What the Blue Book says is already in place

- The Colorado Anti-Discrimination Act covers sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, and gender expression. Public schools may not exclude students from extracurricular activities that align with gender identity. The Blue Book says it is **unclear** how Proposition 134 would affect that statute.
- On February 5, 2025, Executive Order 14201 told federal agencies to read Title IX as barring biological males from female sports. The Blue Book says that in June 2026 the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that Title IX permits states to keep separate male and female sports based on biological sex. It also says at least seven Colorado schools or districts had already adopted restrictions, and that states out of step with federal policy may face funding cuts.
- CHSAA’s rules have recognized transgender participation and allowed review of eligibility decisions. A December 2025 settlement lets schools keep separate teams and facilities by biological sex without a CHSAA sanction. The part of that lawsuit aimed at CADA was still open when the Blue Book was written.
- NCAA rules limit female-sport competition, and the scholarships tied to it, to athletes assigned female at birth. Those NCAA rules do not govern intramurals. Proposition 134 would cover intramurals.

## Official fiscal note

Indeterminate legal cost if the commissioner has to enforce against a district. Colleges update policies at their own expense. Local policy work is expected to be minimal unless enforcement begins.

## Arguments in the Blue Book

**For.** The argument says biological males on female teams take wins, records, and scholarships, and raise injury and locker-room privacy concerns. It wants one statewide rule so athletes in different districts are not under different policies.

**Against.** The argument says the measure creates a body-inspection problem, including for athletes who are not transgender, because schools may choose invasive tests, a doctor’s note, or extra scrutiny of appearance. It says excluding transgender students from the team matching gender identity increases isolation and harm, and that the rule covers young children in intramurals, not only championship sports.

## What can swing a vote

| If this is the priority | It points toward |
| --- | --- |
| One statewide rule that female-designated teams are limited by the reproductive-cell definition | Yes |
| Leaving eligibility to districts, CHSAA, and the NCAA, including the December 2025 settlement already allowing separation without a CHSAA penalty | No |
| A defined, uniform way of verifying sex | The measure does not provide one. If you need a single verification method, this text does not supply it. |
| Private and religious school teams | Covered by the designation rule. The commissioner enforcement path described in the Blue Book is for public K-12 districts. |
| Club and recreation-center sports outside school | Not covered. |
| Conflict with CADA | Unresolved in the Blue Book. A vote either way accepts litigation risk. |
