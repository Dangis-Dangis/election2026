# Amendment 83: Constitutional right to hunt and fish

**On every Denver ballot.** Citizen initiative. Constitutional. Passes with **55 percent**.

[Back to the summary](../ballot-summary.md)

## What the choice does

A yes vote creates a constitutional right to hunt, fish, and harvest wildlife the state manages, by traditional methods. It does not cover nongame species, endangered species, or species illegal to hunt under federal law. It states that hunting and fishing are the preferred means of managing fish and wildlife. The state may still regulate for sound science, public safety, or to preserve future hunting and fishing. Trespass law is unchanged.

The Blue Book staff analysis says there is **no immediate change** to seasons, licenses, methods, or bag limits. The risk it flags is later: courts, the legislature, or the Parks and Wildlife Commission may read the new right as a limit on future rules.

Colorado Parks and Wildlife already manages wildlife under statutes and commission regulations. The “for” argument, not the fiscal note, says about one-third of CPW’s annual revenue, about **$115 million**, comes from license sales, and that 24 other states have a similar constitutional provision.

## Official fiscal note

No significant near-term spending. Legal costs could rise if rules are challenged. The amount “cannot be estimated.”

## Arguments in the Blue Book

**For.** The right would be harder for a future legislature to eliminate. Hunting and fishing are described as heritage and as a tourism and habitat-funding source.

**Against.** Recreation should not be a constitutional right. The “preferred means” clause elevates one management tool. Undefined language could produce lawsuits and weaken rules voters and CPW already set.

## What can swing a vote

| If this is the priority | It points toward |
| --- | --- |
| Making a future ban on hunting or fishing require another constitutional vote | Yes |
| Keeping wildlife policy in statute and commission rules, where it can be changed without a 55 percent campaign | No |
| Protecting license revenue that funds habitat | Yes, if you accept the proponents’ revenue claim as the reason to lock the policy in |
| Worry that “preferred means” will be used to challenge non-hunting tools such as habitat closures or method limits | No |
| Denver-specific effect | Small in daily city policy. The legal effect is statewide wildlife management, including lands outside Denver. |
