# Denver City Council At-Large, Seat B

**On every Denver ballot.** Special municipal election held with the general election. Nonpartisan. Vote for one.

[Back to the summary](../ballot-summary.md)

## Why the seat is open

On August 5, 2026, City Council declared a vacancy in At-Large Seat B. The Denver Elections Division’s vacancy page sets the election for November 3 and swearing-in for November 24, after certification.

News accounts identify the departing member as Sarah Parady. The Kim Monson site’s November 2026 election brief, citing Colorado Politics, says she announced on June 1 that medical challenges and overlapping chronic illnesses had made it hard to attend meetings, and that she resigned August 5. That health account is a news report of her stated reason, not a city medical finding.

The same brief, citing Axios Denver, says the winner serves the rest of the term **through July 19, 2027**, and that the person with the most votes wins **without a runoff**. The next regular municipal election is in 2027, so this term is the bridge, on the order of eight months after swearing-in.

## What can swing a vote

| If this is the priority | What to weigh |
| --- | --- |
| Who holds a citywide vote on zoning, the budget, public safety, and homelessness until July 2027 | Compare the ten statements. This guide does not rank them. |
| Party label | The ballot does not print one. Reyes’s public comment was that he wants the council kept nonpartisan. Other candidates may still be party actors. That was not checked one by one. |
| A runoff if nobody reaches 50 percent | The news brief says there is no runoff. First place wins. In a ten-person field, first place can be well below half the vote. |
| The Xcel franchise, Question 2K | Council already voted to put 2K on this ballot, 9–2, with Lewis and Parady voting no, according to the Kano Project’s October 6, 2026 account. The new member does not get that referral vote. They would be seated for whatever comes after certification. |
| Polling | None located. A ten-way plurality race can be decided by a small share of the vote. Absence of a poll is a larger gap here than in a two-person race. |
